SIMMA method

One library prep, split in two — four signals out of a single WGS run

Precision matters here: "multimodal from one sample" is one prep then split into two aliquots, not a single aliquot, and the targeted branch still uses PCR. Everything below is the paper's own assertion.

Thesis premise

The bottleneck is not how deep short-read looks — it is what looking costs in material

The framing "short-read cannot dig into plasma biology, therefore ONT" was rejected: nearly everything known about cfDNA biology was found with short reads, and short reads beat ONT on per-base accuracy. Scale below: 1 ng = 80 px.

The claim that dies in 30 seconds
"Short-read cannot dig into the biology of plasma, so we need ONT."
Short reads found the 167 bp nucleosome periodicity, tumour-fragment shortening, end motifs and tissue-of-origin — and cfDNA is only 167 bp, so paired-end reads cover the whole molecule. ONT's read-length advantage is nearly useless here.
The claim that holds
"With a few nanograms, short-read forces a choice — either mutation or methylation, never both on the same molecule. ONT does not force that choice."
A single fragment carrying a mutation and a tumour methylation pattern and anomalous length has a near-zero chance of being noise. That linkage is what is lost when the tube is split.
Short-read's own record — do not write it off
It found the 167 bp nucleosome periodicity, that tumour cfDNA runs 10–20 bp shorter, tumour-vs-normal end motifs, tissue-of-origin from methylation — and it counts mutations at 1-in-10,000 more accurately than ONT does.
Must be named in the thesis, or a reviewer will name it for me: EM-seq (enzymatic, far gentler than bisulfite) and Duet / TAPS (retain mutation and methylation together). The argument survives, but only if it moves to: even those give no end-motif and native fragment length at single-molecule resolution.
Thesis premise

ecDNA is not a 24th chromosome — and the word "chromosome" deletes the whole argument

My working model was "one of the 23 chromosomes gets torn off and becomes a free 24th chromosome". About 70% right: origin and lawlessness are correct, the rest is not — and the part that is wrong is the part that matters.

The sentence that can go into the thesis
ecDNA is a circular, acentric DNA element assembled from fragments of one or more chromosomes (chromothripsis); lacking a centromere it segregates randomly at mitosis, so copy number per cell is a random variable from 0 to thousands. ecDNA-derived molecules enter plasma as ctDNA.
The trap in the paper — do not get this backwards
"ecDNA is 1 Mb, so long reads read the whole circle" is wrong, and SIMMA says so itself: in cfDNA ecDNA is fragmented like chromosomal DNA and is not the source of the longest fragments or the hypomethylation. Long reads win by spanning breakpoints inside repeats — and by a clean copy-number spike: >800 MYCN copies at 0.6x coverage, assembly 1.3 Mb / 6 contigs, N50 843,316 bp.
Scope call taken today: this thesis's core contribution is same-molecule co-occurrence of ≥2 variants on one long read — that is not ecDNA. ecDNA stays context (why long reads are worth it, and what SIMMA already claimed), not something to implement. That call saves weeks, and is worth confirming with the professor.
