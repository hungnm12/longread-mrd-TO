Thesis premise

ecDNA is not a 24th chromosome — and the word "chromosome" deletes the whole argument

My working model was "one of the 23 chromosomes gets torn off and becomes a free 24th chromosome". About 70% right: origin and lawlessness are correct, the rest is not — and the part that is wrong is the part that matters.

The sentence that can go into the thesis
ecDNA is a circular, acentric DNA element assembled from fragments of one or more chromosomes (chromothripsis); lacking a centromere it segregates randomly at mitosis, so copy number per cell is a random variable from 0 to thousands. ecDNA-derived molecules enter plasma as ctDNA.
The trap in the paper — do not get this backwards
"ecDNA is 1 Mb, so long reads read the whole circle" is wrong, and SIMMA says so itself: in cfDNA ecDNA is fragmented like chromosomal DNA and is not the source of the longest fragments or the hypomethylation. Long reads win by spanning breakpoints inside repeats — and by a clean copy-number spike: >800 MYCN copies at 0.6x coverage, assembly 1.3 Mb / 6 contigs, N50 843,316 bp.
Scope call taken today: this thesis's core contribution is same-molecule co-occurrence of ≥2 variants on one long read — that is not ecDNA. ecDNA stays context (why long reads are worth it, and what SIMMA already claimed), not something to implement. That call saves weeks, and is worth confirming with the professor.
