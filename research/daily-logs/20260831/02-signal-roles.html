Signal inventory

SNV is the baseline, not an addition — and only one of the other three signals is testable here

The user's proposal was "CN + methylation are the useful signals, and SNV too". The ordering was reversed: the thesis is an SNV counting rule on tumour-only candidates; everything else is an ablation branch asking "does adding this help?". Without that ordering there is no baseline left.

SignalWhat it answers in SIMMARole on this project's tumour/normal dilution data
SNV Not in the tumour-agnostic model at all — split into a separate targeted 76-gene UMI assay (ALK, TP53, MYC, CCND1, ATRX) The primary signal. This is the thesis. A group with 792 real patient samples still separated SNV into its own assay rather than mixing it in — which pre-answers "why not just use SIMMA for the SNV problem?"
Methylation Detects samples where copy number sees nothing; also classifies tumour type. Strongest at satellite repeats, which only long reads can place The only genuinely new, testable addition. MM/ML tags already present on the dilution BAMs, 5mCG_5hmCG — computable without re-running the instrument
Copy number The best quantitative signal — reads tumour fraction off 500 kb bin deviations Positive control only, not evidence. Cell line spiked into its own matched normal means CN tracks the mixing ratio by construction. If it fails to track, data prep is broken — that is all it can tell us
Fragment length An independent third vote; the short-fragment shift is visible even in CN-negative samples Unusable — cell-line DNA is machine-sheared, so length reflects library preparation, not biology. Already recorded in design-space/gaps.md
ecDNA Visible below 0.1× coverage; perfectly specific; per-patient junction fingerprint for longitudinal tracking Out of scope — no ecDNA to track, and present in only 65/792 (8.2%) of SIMMA's own cohort
Takeaway: the paper's title half (ecDNA) is the thinnest evidence and the least usable here; the untitled half — methylation and conformal prediction over all 792 samples — is what this thesis should cite, as a hold-out-scale comparison (43 individuals vs this project's 6).
