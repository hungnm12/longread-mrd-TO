Why methylation survives at low tumour fraction: the denominator changed, not the sample

The same 9 ng of cfDNA at TF = 1e-4, counted two ways. Log axis — each gridline is 100×.

Three things methylation still does NOT fix — all three must be said out loud in the thesis
(a) The sampling limit
It extracts more information per tumour molecule; it does not create tumour molecules. If the tube truly contains zero, methylation reads zero. Only drawing more blood helps.
(b) Cell-of-origin confounding
The mean shift also moves with chemotherapy, marrow suppression and inflammation. Pre-analytical handling matters too — plasma separated up to seven days after venepuncture.
(c) Sample-level, not molecule-level
It says "this sample looks tumour-shifted". It never says "molecule #4,281 is tumour" — which is exactly why SIMMA wraps the output in conformal prediction.
Takeaway: SNV asks for one specific rare event and dies of small n; methylation estimates a mean shift over n ≈ 10⁶, where standard error falls as 1/√n. Same tube, different denominator — not a better assay.
